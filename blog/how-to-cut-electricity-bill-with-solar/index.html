How to Cut Your Electricity Bill With Solar (2026 Guide)

Published Aug 30, 2026 · Last reviewed: October 22, 2026 · Lucas Ballek

The short answer: solar can reduce the electricity you buy from the grid, but no fixed bill-cut percentage or payback period applies everywhere. Your result depends on the electricity you use, when you use it, what the system produces, how your utility credits exports, and the full price of the system. The U.S. Department of Energy lists these as key factors in solar savings; check the rules and rates for your own utility.

A model can compare scenarios, but it cannot inspect your roof, read your utility contract, or promise a financial outcome. Treat every output as an estimate and verify the assumptions against current bills and itemized quotes.

Three levers that affect your bill

  • Use less electricity. Efficiency changes the amount of energy a system needs to cover. The estimator uses the load you enter; it does not model a specific efficiency upgrade for you.
  • Generate electricity on site. Solar can reduce power bought from the grid while panels are producing. Roof direction, shade, weather, equipment, and the hours you use electricity all affect how much of that production offsets your bill.
  • Shift energy to a later time. A battery can store some solar power for later use or backup. The financial value depends on the local rate schedule, export credit, battery cost, and how much power you shift; storage is not automatically the cheaper option.

Use your own bill to compare options

  1. Start with recent utility bills. Note monthly kWh, the rate schedule, fixed charges, and any time-of-use periods. Check the rate and export credit shown in the estimator against your current utility tariff.
  2. Confirm export rules. Compensation for power sent to the grid varies by utility and location. Use the current utility terms for your address rather than assuming every exported kWh earns the retail rate.
  3. Compare like with like. Keep the same load and tariff assumptions while comparing solar-only and solar-plus-storage options, different cut targets, and different budgets. Look at the modeled bill reduction and full cost together.
  4. Check the site separately. Confirm roof condition, orientation, shade, electrical service, permits, inspection, and interconnection with your utility and qualified local professionals. A weather-based estimate does not inspect these conditions.

What the estimate can and cannot tell you

The calculator uses a five-year hourly weather series from NASA POWER with the location and load you enter. NASA describes its Hourly API as time-series solar and meteorological data; it is not a measurement of your roof. NREL's PVWatts guidance also describes modeled output as an estimate with assumptions and uncertainty. The calculator cannot see roof shade, equipment condition, contract terms, or every local charge, so use its displayed assumptions and verify the result before making a purchase decision.

Start with your bill, not a fixed percentage. Use a current location and load, check the utility rate and export credit, then compare the estimate with a complete local quote.

Frequently asked

How much of my electricity bill can solar panels cut?

There is no universal percentage. Solar output, the home's electricity use and timing, roof and site conditions, utility rates, and export credits all affect the result. Use recent utility bills and the current local tariff. The calculator is an estimate, not a roof inspection or savings promise.

Do I need a battery to cut my bill with solar?

A battery can shift solar energy for later use or backup, but it does not guarantee a lower bill. The value depends on your rate schedule, export compensation, installed battery cost, and how much power you shift. Compare solar scenarios with and without storage.

How fast does solar pay for itself?

There is no universal payback period. Use the full price you would actually pay, financing terms, utility bills, export credits, operating and replacement costs, and only incentives you have verified for your location. Treat a calculator result as an estimate, not a promise.

Is it cheaper to build the battery myself?

Not always. Compare like-for-like written quotes for usable capacity, power, protection, enclosure, shipping, installation, permits, inspection, warranty, and support. A cell-only price is not the cost of an installed system. Check local rules and use qualified help for electrical work.
