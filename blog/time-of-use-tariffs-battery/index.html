Time-of-Use Battery Savings: Dodge Peak Prices (2026)

📅 Sep 7, 2026 · Lucas Ballek · 10 min read · every number below comes from this site's deterministic simulator, not guesswork

The short answer: time-of-use tariffs sell the same kWh at two to three prices in one day — and a battery lets you buy low and use high. Shifting just 2 kWh/day out of the evening peak across a typical $0.25–0.35/kWh spread is worth roughly $165–235/year; a 2–4 kWh LFP bank aimed at the 4–9 pm window therefore pays in 1–3 years on strong spreads.

How time-of-use tariffs actually work

  • Off-peak (11 pm–7 am): roughly $0.08–0.20/kWh.
  • Shoulder (daytime): roughly $0.15–0.30/kWh.
  • Peak (4–9 pm): roughly $0.30–0.55/kWh.

The ratio is what matters: peak-to-off-peak ratios of 1.5–3x are typical. Check your utility's tariff sheet for exact windows; the estimator accepts your peak and average rates directly.

Battery arbitrage math: the one formula

Yearly savings ≈ kWh shifted/day × (peak − off-peak) × round-trip efficiency × days shifted/year

  • kWh shifted/day: 2–6 kWh for most homes.
  • Spread: $0.10–0.45/kWh in practice.
  • Round-trip: 0.90–0.92 LFP/sodium-ion, 0.85 lead-acid.
  • Days: 330–365 grid-charged, 280–330 solar-charged.

Worked example: shifting 2 kWh every evening

Off-peak $0.12–0.18/kWh, peak $0.37–0.53/kWh — a $0.25–0.35 spread. A 2 kWh usable LFP bank covering the 5–9 pm load:

  • Gross per day: 2 kWh × $0.25–0.35 = $0.50–0.70/day.
  • After 0.92 round-trip: $0.46–0.64/day.
  • Yearly (330–365 days): roughly $150–235/year.
  • Bank cost: 2 kWh × $80–125/kWh DIY = $160–250; retail $220–330.
  • Simple payback: 1–2 years DIY, 1–3 years retail.

At a thin $0.10–0.15 spread the same bank earns only $65–100/year. Identical hardware, 3–4x different income — type your real rates into the simulator, not an average.

Solar-charged vs grid-charged battery

A solar-charged bank stores midday surplus and deploys it into a $0.30–0.55 peak — the fastest payback (1–3 years). A grid-charged bank charges off-peak at $0.08–0.20 and works 330–365 days/year in any weather, needs no panels, and costs only bank plus inverter. Renters and shaded roofs: the TOU-only path is yours.

The worst-week caveat

Every size on this site is checked against the darkest week of five years of NASA hourly weather. A solar-charged bank sized for April coasts through summer, then a December murk arrives with 20–40% of normal harvest. Expect solar-charged TOU savings to dip 30–60% in your worst month; grid-charged savings do not dip at all.

When TOU arbitrage does NOT pay

  • Spread under $0.10/kWh. Shifting 2–4 kWh/day earns $65–150/year — a $320–660 bank stretches past 5 years.
  • Lead-acid on daily duty. 500 field cycles means a fresh bank every 1–2 years; lifetime cost $2,270–3,240 per usable kWh.
  • Demand charges instead of TOU. That game needs power (kW of inverter), not energy (kWh of bank).
  • Flat-rate net metering. Where exports earn near-retail, the grid is already a free battery.

Frequently asked

How much can a battery save on a time-of-use tariff?

Roughly $75–150/year per kWh shifted daily: 2 kWh across a typical $0.25–0.35 spread earns $150–235/year after round-trip loss.

What size battery do I need to avoid peak prices?

Cover your 4–9 pm load: 2–4 kWh usable for most 10 kWh/day homes, 4–6 kWh for heavy evening users. Past 6 kWh returns diminish hard.

Can a battery save money without solar panels?

Yes on TOU rates — grid-charging off-peak and discharging at peak works 330–365 days/year. On flat power it rarely pays.

Does cloudy weather break TOU battery savings?

Only solar-charged savings dip (30–60% worst month). Grid-charged savings are weather-proof.
