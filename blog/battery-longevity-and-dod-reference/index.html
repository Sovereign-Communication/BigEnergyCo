Battery cycle-life guide

Battery cycle life and depth of discharge: a practical guide

By Lucas Ballek · BigEnergyCo Engineering Published: Sep 11, 2026 Reading Time: 14 min
What the evidence supports

No single cycle count or service life applies to every battery sold under the same chemistry label. Cell design and test conditions matter. Use the product data sheet and the limits stated by the battery maker and battery-management system when planning a bank.

1. Cycle-life ratings belong to a test protocol

Depth of discharge (DoD) describes how much of a battery's rated capacity is removed during a discharge. It is one input to aging, not a stand-alone lifetime predictor. A Sandia study tested commercial LFP, NCA, and NMC cells at different discharge rates, depths of discharge, and temperatures. It found substantial variation in time and cycle counts to 80% capacity, including variation among cells of the same chemistry. The result describes those cells and protocols; it does not establish a universal cycle-life curve for every product. See Preger et al. (2020).

A cycle-life number is useful only alongside the conditions under which it was measured. When comparing products, record the DoD or state-of-charge window, temperature, charge and discharge rate, voltage limits, cycle profile, and the maker's end-of-life criterion. NREL's operation and maintenance guide emphasizes documenting operating conditions when evaluating energy-storage systems.

2. What a larger battery can and cannot tell you

If the energy taken from a bank stays the same, increasing its usable capacity reduces the share used on a typical day. That is capacity arithmetic, not a promise of a particular cycle count, calendar life, or replacement date. Temperature, charge settings, system controls, and time in service still matter.

For an initial sizing estimate, divide the energy that must come from the battery by the usable fraction selected for the scenario. Then account for conversion losses, reserve, and the limits in the battery and inverter documentation. Treat the result as a planning input; it is not a warranty or a battery-life prediction.

3. Chemistry does not guarantee a service life

Cycle-life figures describe a particular cell or pack under a stated test. Chemistry alone does not establish usable capacity, cycle life, calendar life, cold-weather performance, or replacement timing. Read the exact product documentation and compare the conditions behind each rating.

Lithium iron phosphate (LFP)

A cycle count is meaningful only with its test conditions and end-of-life definition. Preger et al. tested commercial lithium-ion cells under different cycling conditions and found substantial variation in the time and cycle count to 80% capacity. That study describes its tested cells; it is not a universal curve for every LFP product. See the study record.

For a product comparison, check the maker's stated charge and discharge limits, temperature, rate, depth of discharge, and capacity threshold. Do not convert a lab cycle count directly into a calendar service-life promise.

Sodium-ion

Do not infer a pack's usable range, voltage limits, storage behavior, cold-weather limits, or inverter compatibility from the chemistry name. Follow the exact battery manufacturer's operating limits, battery-management-system cutoffs, and the inverter maker's compatibility guidance. Treat any cycle-life figure as specific to the product and test conditions that produced it.

Lead-acid and AGM

Cycle life also depends on the particular model and its use. For example, Discover's AGM performance sheet says cyclic life depends on capacity taken, discharge rate, temperature, and charging method. Its cycle-life chart gives the test current, final voltage, charge current, and temperature. Those results describe the listed test, not every AGM battery. See the manufacturer's performance sheet.

Across chemistries, use the exact model's warranty and maintenance instructions, and plan around the loads, environment, and service conditions you expect. A cycle count alone cannot establish how many years a home battery will last.

6. Build a cost comparison from stated inputs

Lifetime-cost comparisons depend on the exact products, their usable energy, installed price, warranty, operating conditions, and replacement assumptions. A cycle count alone does not establish a replacement date or total cost.

Inputs to disclose in a battery cost comparison
Input What to state
Battery product Exact cell or pack model, nameplate capacity, and usable capacity.
Price Source and date, currency, tax, shipping, and installation.
Test and service conditions Temperature, discharge rate, depth of discharge, and end-of-life capacity.
Replacement estimate Source for cycle-life or calendar-life assumptions, plus labor, freight, and recycling.
Comparison method Shared analysis period and any efficiency or discount assumptions.
Uncertain values Label estimates and unknowns instead of presenting them as product facts.

Mark unavailable prices or service-life inputs as assumptions. Do not present a scenario as a guaranteed saving or a universal battery replacement schedule.

7. Builder’s Field Guide: Sizing & Inverter Configuration Checklist

To implement a true zero-swap storage strategy in your own installation:

  1. Calculate True Daily Nighttime Consumption: Determine your average consumption between solar sunset and sunrise. Add 15% inverter standby and conversion overhead.
  2. Apply the Zero-Swap Oversize Factor:
    • For LFP: Target an installed nameplate capacity equal to Daily Usable kWh ÷ 0.65.
    • For Sodium-Ion: Target an installed nameplate capacity equal to Daily Usable kWh ÷ 0.70.
  3. Program Inverter Voltage Cutoffs Precisely:
    • For 16S LFP (48V nominal): Set Float to 54.0V (3.375V/cell), Bulk/Absorption to 56.0V (3.50V/cell), and Low Voltage Cutoff (LVD) to 48.0V (3.00V/cell). Avoiding 3.65V top-charging eliminates high-voltage electrolyte oxidation.
    • For 16S Na-ion (48V nominal): Set Bulk to 57.6V (3.60V/cell), Float to 54.4V (3.40V/cell), and Low Voltage Cutoff to 40.0V (2.50V/cell).
  4. Honor Cold Temperature Thresholds: If ambient winter temperatures drop below 0°C (32°F), select Sodium-ion to eliminate the need for parasitic heating pads, or house LFP inside the conditioned thermal envelope of the building.

8. Frequently Asked Questions

How should I compare cycle-life ratings at different DoD values?
Compare ratings only when their test conditions and end-of-life criteria are stated. Depth of discharge is one variable; cell design, temperature, charge and discharge rate, and voltage limits also matter. Research on commercial cells reports variation across cycling conditions, so a generic cycle count is not a life guarantee.
Can a sodium-ion battery be discharged to 0 V?
There is no chemistry-wide cell-voltage cutoff that can be treated as a normal discharge target. The permitted lower voltage and instructions after over-discharge depend on the exact cell or pack. Follow that product’s data sheet and battery-management-system limits; do not infer a safe endpoint from the chemistry name.
How much of a sodium-ion battery can a standard inverter use?
There is no chemistry-wide usable percentage. Usable energy depends on the exact cell or pack voltage range, inverter low-voltage settings, and battery-management system. CATL describes dedicated voltage regulation and a sodium-specific battery-management system for its TENER Sodium system; that product-specific design does not establish a cutoff or usable fraction for other packs. Check the manuals and compatibility information for the exact battery and inverter before pairing them.
Can an oversized AGM battery bank be expected to last 20 years?
Do not infer a 20-year service life or fixed replacement count from bank size alone. Service depends on the exact model and duty cycle. Discover’s Standard AGM Performance data sheet says stand-by life depends on frequency and depth of discharge, charge voltage, and ambient temperature; cyclic life also depends on capacity taken, discharge rate, operating temperature, and charging method. Use the exact product data sheet and label any projection as an estimate.
Does oversizing a battery bank always save money?
No. A larger bank may change operating depth of discharge for a given load, but that alone does not establish cycle life, service life, or lifetime cost. Compare the exact battery model, usable capacity, installed price, warranty, operating conditions, replacement assumptions, and analysis period using the inputs listed above. Do not claim a saving or replacement schedule without sourced assumptions.
